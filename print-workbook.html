How to use this workbook

This workbook follows the interactive study. It is divided into clearly marked parts:

  • Original prophetic direction: the declarations and directions exactly as spoken in the service.
  • Supplementary biblical study: lessons, word studies and commentary. These are study helps, not the minister's words.
  • Personalized prayers: family prayers built from the King James Scripture used in the service.
  • Interactive quizzes: questions with an answer key at the back.

All Scripture is quoted from the King James Version. Words in [square brackets] were unclear in the recording; the bracketed word is a suggested reading.

Beginner lessons

Simple words. One truth at a time. Every verse is King James Version.

Lesson 1. Visitation means God steps in

“A visitation is a time of timely intervention, divine visitation. ... is a supernatural and and personal moment when God actively steps into human affairs to manifest His power, presence, or purpose.”Direct quotation · Prophet Prince Amo David, service of Friday, October 16, 2026

In the Bible, when the LORD visited Sarah, He came and did what He had promised. Sarah had a son.

Key point: God keeps His word. Genesis 21:1 says He did unto Sarah "as he had spoken."

Genesis 21:1 (KJV)
1And the LORD visited Sarah as he had said, and the LORD did unto Sarah as he had spoken.

Lesson 1. Peter rescued, James killed

Context. Speaker: Luke, the writer of Acts, records the account. Setting: Herod the king had killed James the brother of John and put Peter in prison during the days of unleavened bread. Many were gathered praying at the house of Mary, the mother of John Mark (Acts 12:12).

In the service, Prophet Prince taught that Peter was rescued because the church prayed. The chapter itself places the church's prayer (verse 5) right before the angel's rescue (verse 7).

“One thing that magnified his deliverance was that it was a signal answer to prayer”Matthew Henry · Matthew Henry's Commentary, Acts 12
“for prayers and tears are the church's arms; therewith she fights, not only against her enemies, but for her friends”Matthew Henry · Matthew Henry's Commentary, Acts 12

Both Abraham (Genesis 17:17) and Sarah (Genesis 18:12) laughed at the promise. When it came true, Sarah's laughter became joy (Genesis 21:6).

Answer key

Beginner 1: A. Praying without ceasing unto God for him. Acts 12:5 says prayer was made without ceasing of the church unto God for him. The next verses show God answering by sending His angel. Acts 12:5-7 (KJV)
Beginner 2: D. The angel of the Lord. Acts 12:7 says the angel of the Lord came upon him, a light shined in the prison, and his chains fell off from his hands. Acts 12:7-10 (KJV)
Beginner 3: B. In the tent door in the heat of the day. Genesis 18:1 says he sat in the tent door in the heat of the day. When he saw the three men, he ran to meet them. Genesis 18:1-3 (KJV)
Beginner 4: B. Laughter. Strong's H3327 gives "laughter." Sarah said, "God hath made me to laugh." ("God hears" is the meaning given for Ishmael, and "prince of God" relates to Israel.) Genesis 21:6-7 (KJV)
Beginner 5: A. An heritage of the LORD. Children are an heritage of the LORD, and the fruit of the womb is his reward. Verse 5 adds that the man whose quiver is full of them is happy. Psalm 127:3-5 (KJV)
Intermediate 1: C. Keep praying earnestly to God for them, as the church did. The church prayed without ceasing while Peter was kept in prison. In verse 11 Peter said the Lord had sent His angel and delivered him. The pattern is steady, earnest prayer to God. Acts 12:5-11 (KJV)
Intermediate 2: C. He ran to meet them, bowed, and asked them not to pass away from him. Genesis 18:2-3: he ran to meet them, bowed himself toward the ground, and said, "pass not away, I pray thee, from thy servant." He did not let the moment pass by. Genesis 18:1-5 (KJV)
Intermediate 3: A. He asked, "Is any thing too hard for the LORD?" and set a time for the promise. Genesis 18:14: "Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time appointed I will return unto thee ... and Sarah shall have a son." The doubt did not cancel the promise. Genesis 18:9-14 (KJV)
Intermediate 4: D. By hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Keeping the Word in the ear and in the mouth (Romans 10:8) is how faith is fed. Romans 10:17 (KJV)
Intermediate 5: A. Through faith, because she judged him faithful who had promised. Her faith rested on the character of God: she judged Him faithful who had promised. Strong's G4103, pistos, means trustworthy. Hebrews 11:11 (KJV)
Intermediate 6: A. Immediately he was made whole, took up his bed, and walked. John 5:9 says immediately the man was made whole. Thirty-eight years of waiting ended the moment the word of Jesus came. John 5:5-9 (KJV)
Intermediate 7: C. He rose early, sanctified them, and offered burnt offerings according to the number of them all. Job rose up early in the morning and offered for each child. Verse 5 ends: "Thus did Job continually." Covering children in prayer was his regular practice, not a one-time act. Job 1:5 (KJV)
Advanced 1: D. God attends to His word and acts on what He has spoken. Paqad means to visit, attend to, or care for. Verse 1 links the visitation twice to God's word: "as he had said" and "as he had spoken." Verse 2 adds "at the set time." Genesis 21:1-3 (KJV)
Advanced 2: D. Intent and fervent, stretched out toward God. Ektenes means intent or fervent. Matthew Henry writes that it was "fervent prayer" and that "times of public distress and danger should be praying times with the church." Acts 12:5-7 (KJV)
Advanced 3: D. A people can fail to recognize the time when God draws near, and suffer loss. Episkope means inspection or visitation. Jerusalem's loss came "because thou knewest not the time of thy visitation." Compare Abraham, who recognized the moment and ran to meet it (Genesis 18:2). Luke 19:44 (KJV)
Advanced 4: C. He staggered not through unbelief, was strong in faith, and was fully persuaded God was able to perform. "Fully persuaded" translates plerophoreo (G4135), to be completely assured. Abraham's attention moved from his body to God's ability. Romans 4:17-21 (KJV)
Advanced 5: D. The blood of the Lamb, the word of their testimony, and loving not their lives unto the death. Revelation 12:11 names all three. An accuser brings charges; the answer is the blood of the Lamb and a testimony held firmly. Revelation 12:10-12 (KJV)
Advanced 6: D. Children are an inheritance from the LORD, entrusted to the family. Matthew Henry writes that children "are so to be accounted, blessings and not burdens." Psalm 127:1 also reminds the household that except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain. Psalm 127:1-5 (KJV)
Advanced 7: C. Israel, because as a prince he had power with God and with men, and had prevailed. Strong's H3478 relates Israel to "God prevails." The change of name marked a change of identity after Jacob wrestled and would not let go. Genesis 32:27-28 (KJV)
Advanced 8: C. She asked her husband to build Elisha a little chamber with a bed, table, stool, and candlestick. Her honor was practical and generous. The service named Elijah; 2 Kings 4:8-9 in the King James text names Elisha. 2 Kings 4:8-10 (KJV)
Advanced 9: C. He cast salt into the spring, and the LORD said, "I have healed these waters". 2 Kings 2:21: "there shall not be from thence any more death or barren land." The same place that carried a curse received healing by the word of the LORD. 2 Kings 2:19-22 (KJV)
Advanced 10: C. Turning the heart of the fathers to the children, and the heart of the children to their fathers. The verse describes restoration in both directions: fathers to children and children to fathers. It is a fitting word for family prayer. Malachi 4:6 (KJV)
